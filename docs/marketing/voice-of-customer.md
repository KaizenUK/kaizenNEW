## 1 Oct 2026: founder interview (Sean), question bank

Source: Sean, answering the founder question bank in chat. Sean's words are kept as written (verbatim unless marked). These describe real clients (Midland Oil Group, Helen Moore Hairdressing, Google reviewers) as Sean remembers them; they are evidence of who buys and why, not quotes from the clients.

**1. Who you speak to, who decides.** "Always the owner of the company. Sometimes marketing would have some form of input but generally MD's, they also make the final calls."

**2. What kinds of businesses, how big.** "Salon in wirral with a decent size team. Midland is bigger with pretty big site in Halesowen and fleets of drivers and loads of infrastructure, maybe 20 people there?"

**3. What they say first.** "Looks really dated and they wanted to be brought into the modern day. Sites would load slow or looked cookie cutter, were not getting calls, had no presence in some cases, depended on socials. Don't like the aesthetic that kind of thing."

Phrases worth reusing (customer language): "looks really dated", "brought into the modern day", "loads slow", "cookie cutter", "not getting calls", "no presence", "depended on socials".

**4. Who looked after the site before.** "Sometimes a generic marketing agency who done SEO combined with their work, sometimes a friend, sometimes hack jobs from WP (as in hacked together not computer hack). They felt they were getting ripped off and getting nothing in return of their investment. Some companies have been furious. Some just don't understand it all."

**5. What worried them most about going ahead** (offered: cost, being ripped off again, time, losing what they had, not understanding the tech). "All of the above."

Notes: the previous supplier is usually a marketing agency selling SEO, a friend, or a patched-together WordPress site. The feeling is being "ripped off" and "getting nothing in return", sometimes "furious", sometimes confused. This is the main objection to answer: trust after a bad experience.

**5b. The two worries that come up most.** "Having to pay a lot of money yet again and then it being bad after that as well. Getting used to another type of tech was prob 2nd." On the second: "People don't realise you can have react and still have easily editable blogs for example with sanity. (Dont mention that specifically otherwise people will copy us)"

**6. What Kaizen does, as Sean says it out loud.** "We take old dated websites and make them modern day, super fast, smart, professional and much more adaptable to the modern world. We can and do edit for them fast. We remove the developer to product barrier for them and soon we will have the builder too so they will be able to manage a lot of the content without the need for developer intervention."

**7. When they lean in.** "Usually when they see how much faster a 1st draft has loaded and it looks really nice, clean, performs well - sometimes it can be a feature they didn't know they could have, added in (like the MOG AI). Speedscore doesn't really matter and is very technical to most businesses, its more about seeing the results than seeing a number like that."

Notes:
- Worry 1: paying a lot again and it still being bad. Worry 2: having to learn a new system to edit the site.
- **Rule (Sean):** never name the tech combination that makes fast sites easy to edit (React with Sanity, or similar) in public copy. Say what the customer gets ("you can edit your own pages and blog easily"), not how.
- The moment that sells is **seeing it**: a first draft that loads fast and looks clean, or a feature they did not know was possible (Midland's AI oil finder). Scores and numbers are "very technical" to most owners. Show results, demote scores.

**3b. What "brought into the modern day" means.** "They mean they want better tech stack and not dragged down by monolithic things like WP. Their competitors are changing and they were not keeping up. they now often are leaps ahead." (Sean's paraphrase of clients. On the site this becomes plain words: their competitors moved on, their site held them back, now they are ahead. No "tech stack" or "monolithic".)

**8. Reasons for "no" or "not yet".** "couldnt tell you to be fair as these project we have done have just landed on us, not through pitching (this is what we are changing) - I would imagine it would be cost though or 'we just got it did a few years ago'." (Hypothesis, not evidence: cost, and "we only had it done a few years ago".)

**9. What they compare Kaizen with.** "Doing it themselves, cheaper agencies or social media. People often think either of these are enough but they are really not."

**10. Price.** "Not had that conversation yet so steep that in research maybe?" No evidence yet; needs research before any price appears on the site.

Notes:
- **Rule (Sean): never use jargon.** Not on pages, headings, buttons or FAQs. Words currently on the site that break it include "tech stack", "monolithic", "Core Web Vitals", "INP", "Cumulative Layout Shift", "information architecture", "crawl budget", "schema", "data markup", "decoupled", "headless", "frontend", "Lighthouse". Say what it does for the owner instead.
- Work so far has come by referral, not pitching. The site's new job is to win work from people who do not already know Sean, and to reassure referrals who check the site before getting in touch.
- The real competition: doing it yourself, a cheaper agency, or just social media. The page has to show why those are not enough, without sneering at them.

**11. What Midland Oil gets day to day.** Sean: Midland "get much more qualified leads to their site now and has even had an offer from a large company that was worth a lot of money to them which they never would have before." "The leads they get are several a day and are genuine, proper leads now instead of the vanity traffic they used to get from the old developers site." "Just be careful how you word it - we wouldnt want them to look incapable."
- **Confidential (Sean):** the large company's name is private. Never name it, and never say the order was turned down or why. Safe wording: "Since the rebuild, Midland gets several genuine enquiries a day, and has had interest from a large national business it would not have reached before." Check with Midland before this line goes live; it is a new claim beyond the agreed case-study figures.
- Customer language: "qualified leads", "genuine, proper leads", "vanity traffic" (the last is jargon; on the site say "visits that never turned into enquiries").

**13. Who replies, how fast.** "I do, same day or max 1 working day." Site line (Sean asked not to be named): "One of us will get back to you the same day, or the next working day at the latest."

**14. Photo.** "No photo of me - go off midlands story." The About page leads with the work and Midland's story, not a face.

**12. First draft before paying?** "No, they would pay a non-refundable deposit first." So there is no free first draft or mock-up offer. The free, low-commitment step stays the speed check and a conversation; the site must not imply a free design.
