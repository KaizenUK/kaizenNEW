# Builder billing

**Implemented and locally verified — L6-T2, 15 September 2026.** Account and website billing screens, Stripe Checkout/portal/webhook handling, billing ownership, project/page/storage/publication limits and repository source/preview/deployment accounting are complete for this task. Original-site publication and output recovery finish atomically after verification. Provider activation and deployment remain part of the coordinated L6 rollout; production remains on L5. Physical upload/storage reservations and abuse controls follow in L6-T4.

## Product choices

One account pays for its websites. Invited access does not consume the invited person's plan. Projects can have multiple owners, so a private mapping records the billing owner. New websites use their first owner's plan; existing websites use a deterministic existing owner with private-beta access. New accounts start on Free. No migration creates a paid subscription or charges a card.

| Plan         | Websites | Pages per website | Storage across websites | Publishes per calendar month |
| ------------ | -------: | ----------------: | ----------------------: | ---------------------------: |
| Free         |        1 |                 5 |                 100 MiB |                           10 |
| Plus         |        3 |                50 |                   2 GiB |                          100 |
| Agency       |       20 |               200 |                  20 GiB |                        1,000 |
| Private beta |      100 |             1,000 |                 100 GiB |                       10,000 |

The recommended initial monthly catalogue is Plus at £19 and Agency at £59. These are product choices for provider setup, not claims that live prices exist. The screen reads the amount and tax behavior from the configured Stripe prices. It never substitutes a fixture or hard-coded amount when provider verification fails. The final Checkout total is authoritative.

Cancellation and downgrade preserve saved content. New usage must fit the current limits. `active` and `trialing` subscriptions with a recognised price retain their plan until their period ends. `past_due`, `unpaid`, `paused`, incomplete or cancelled subscriptions do not grant paid entitlements. An unrecognised price or quantity requires review and cannot silently grant a paid plan.

## Website billing and usage

Settings → Website plan and usage shows the site's plan, page count and website storage. Only its billing owner sees their account's aggregate websites, storage and monthly publication usage. Other members cannot read usage from that owner's other websites. An existing owner may explicitly confirm **Use my plan for this website**. The server binds the request to verified Auth, rechecks ownership and moves the website only if it fits the receiver's plan. The action does not buy or change a subscription. No owner can assign a charge to another account. Pending publication or recovery must finish first; removing or demoting the current payer requires another owner to take billing first.

Account and per-project locks protect counters across concurrent creates, transfers, workspace saves and publication requests. Archived websites count. Page and registered-asset increases beyond the allowance are rejected transactionally. Existing over-limit work survives a downgrade and can be edited without increasing the exceeded usage; publishing requires the current plan to fit. The original site's separate page/asset tables remain authoritative for its usage, rather than its compatibility workspace. An existing owned website without a saved draft is backfilled with zero usage.

Publication requests reserve an allowance in the billing owner's UTC calendar month. Verified live jobs consume it once; failed/rolled-back requests return an unused reservation once. Rollback and taking a website offline remain available when the allowance is exhausted. Reviewed client snapshots are checked again when a queued job advances, preventing a smaller current workspace from concealing an oversized earlier review. Operator recovery can restore a previously failed artifact without a quota preventing recovery; its original month is charged once.

The repository helper now reserves a publication allowance before Git starts. Its private receipt binds the website, actor, repository configuration, reviewed/base commits and attempt to a private database row. Only signed helper observations tied to verified Auth can reserve or settle it. Lost Git acknowledgements retain the reservation; elapsed time alone never refunds it. A definite failure before Git can return an unused allowance, including when a reserve response is lost. An explicit retry uses the current payer and UTC month, and stale attempts cannot settle the newer attempt. A live publication consumes its allowance once. Pending repository publication also blocks a billing transfer or helper reconfiguration.

**L6-T4 boundary:** the release worker accounts for staging/production output and native Publish requires the measured staged artifact and commit. Registered asset bytes are not a physical Storage quota: retained objects, direct/resumable uploads and unregistered files require the reservations, verified object metadata and cleanup planned for L6-T4. Do not describe these paths as fully limited until their implementation and tests pass.

## Existing website source and preview usage

`builder-repository-usage.mjs` measures actual source bytes, including untracked files, and projects the reviewed replacement bytes before applying a change. Dependencies, generated output and recovery directories keep their separate operational disk/retention limits. A source measurement is bound to the same content fingerprint used by the runner. The helper cannot accept a browser-supplied byte count. Before mutation, it records observed existing usage and atomically reserves growth through the signed billing connection. A lost response stops the apply; a fresh explicit proposal reconciles actual files before reserving again. Typed quota and service errors remain intact when no source mutation occurred.

The runner counts HTML pages and bytes in the actual frozen preview output before exposing it. One dynamic source route generating six HTML pages counts as six pages. A denied check uses the existing output recovery path and leaves no usable new preview. The hosted queue rechecks the captured actor's access before this check. A smaller explicit rebuild can succeed after an oversized build is refused.

Migration `202609150005_builder_repository_usage.sql` combines repository storage with registered managed assets under the account lock. A version fence rejects a delayed observation or reservation after a newer one. Billing transfer and project removal move/remove the repository counters too; managed workspace saves cannot reset them. Observed over-limit data after a downgrade remains recorded and intact. New growth is refused, and publishing/previewing must fit. Accounting uses the largest measured source copy plus the largest measured output copy and the greater of managed and rendered page counts. Pending and held recovery measurements stay included until verified replacement; managed saves cannot clear them. A smaller source/preview/staged/production candidate may fit after a downgrade while older copies remain counted until replaced. Other websites still see the conservative account total. Direct/resumable upload and retained physical-storage enforcement remain L6-T4.

## Staging and production output usage

`builder-repository-output.mjs` connects both ordinary repository deployment and the original-site coordinator to a private output ledger. Each reservation binds a fixed website/channel, request ID, retained artifact, source commit, manifest digest, source measurement, output bytes and HTML pages. Source is measured before and after building; the verified retained manifest supplies output counts. Native Publish also carries the reviewed staging artifact ID and requires its exact commit to be the current verified staging measurement.

Only a verified switch replaces the live measurement. A definite pre-switch failure or verified rollback returns the pending bytes; lost acknowledgements retain them. A failed-before-start tombstone prevents a delayed reservation from activating later. Explicit recovery holds older uncertain attempts, rejects their late settlement and keeps their bytes counted until the recovered website passes served-output verification. Billing transfer is unavailable while output or recovery remains pending; an archive does not prevent finishing an already-owned recovery.

The recovery command reuses the existing stopped-process filesystem recovery lock. A retained artifact without a ledger is measured before restoration; an existing mismatched ledger is refused. Every recovery uses a fresh usage reservation so the stopped worker cannot settle the recovered attempt. Recovery remains possible after archival.

For the original-site coordinator, the same command checks the exact publication request, baseline and retained activation journal before taking new database ownership. It fences the old worker, refuses unrelated artifacts or a changed publication head, and cannot roll back an already committed publication without a new reviewed rollback. After served-output verification, publication promotion or refund and usage settlement share one transaction. Failure keeps both pending; a lost acknowledgement is safely retryable. Newer drafts survive. Ordinary repository recovery finishes the output ledger without a managed publication queue. See [release operations](website-releases.md#l6-output-usage-configuration-implementation-pending-rollout).

Physical retained files, dependencies, caches and uploads remain the separate L6-T4 enforcement scope.

## Payment and recovery flow

Account → Plan and billing offers Checkout for a first subscription and Manage billing for an existing one. Payment details stay on Stripe. Returning to `?view=account&billing=returned` triggers a server refresh; the URL cannot confirm payment or assign a plan. Sign-in is checked before and after a client request, and stale replies are discarded after account changes or unmounting.

Customer binding uses the immutable Auth account ID, not email matching. A stored attempt supplies the customer idempotency key. A provider metadata lookup recovers a customer created before a lost response. Unresolved attempts outside Stripe's idempotency retention window require reconciliation rather than creating another customer blindly. Merely opening the billing panel does not start that attempt.

Each unfinished Checkout has one durable attempt, price, fixed expiry and provider session ID. A retry reuses that attempt and checks for an existing provider session. The same idempotency key is reused even after a transport failure. Selecting a different plan requires closing the earlier Checkout. A local deadline alone does not prove a known provider session is closed. An unbound, expired attempt may be abandoned only after a complete provider listing finds no matching session; a concurrent binding prevents that transition.

The webhook verifies the bounded raw body with the official pinned `stripe@22.6.2` SDK and endpoint signing secret. It rejects Connect/organisation or opposite-mode deliveries. Supported subscription and Checkout events trigger a fresh provider read. A private event ledger deduplicates completed events. A customer-scoped lease binds the worker to one delivery; only that unexpired lease may commit the fetched subscription snapshot and Checkout state. Delayed events never overwrite state from their embedded payload, and expired workers cannot commit or release a newer lease. Provider or database failures remain retryable non-success responses.

The account deletion workflow refuses to complete while an unfinished Checkout or nonterminal subscription remains, or while provider state is stale. A subscription scheduled to cancel later is still a subscription until it ends. The Account screen explains closing Checkout, ending subscriptions, refreshing billing and having another owner take over website billing before deletion.

## Provider configuration for the L6 rollout

Server-only settings are `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`, `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `BUILDER_STRIPE_LIVE_MODE`, `BUILDER_STRIPE_PRICE_PLUS`, `BUILDER_STRIPE_PRICE_AGENCY`, `BUILDER_STRIPE_PORTAL_CONFIGURATION` and the canonical HTTPS `PUBLIC_SITE_ORIGIN`. Do not place credentials in frontend settings. The two price IDs must be different, active GBP monthly licensed prices in the selected mode. The portal configuration must permit invoice/payment-method management, appropriate plan changes and cancellation, using only the intended catalogue. Verify its actual configuration at activation.

Deploy `builder-billing` and `builder-billing-webhook` with platform JWT verification disabled: the first verifies Auth itself and the second verifies the Stripe signature. Both retain server request limits. Subscribe the endpoint to `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated`, `customer.subscription.deleted`, `checkout.session.completed` and `checkout.session.expired`. The API has no client-selected customer, price ID, return URL or portal configuration.

Six data migrations add ten private tables, repository/recovery columns and service-only RPCs. All ten are included in the complete 37-table access-matrix fixture, which checks permissive platform defaults, RLS, direct SQL privileges and function execution. The seventh migration, `202609150008_builder_billing_retention.sql`, schedules processed-event cleanup daily at 04:17 in the database scheduler's timezone. Only completed deliveries older than 90 days are removed; unresolved deliveries remain.

Add the `billing-processing` check from `deploy/ops/monitor.example.json` when deploying the updated operations runtime. It reads aggregate counts of delayed billing observations, held output, original-site recovery and cleanup failures. Cleanup is healthy only when the intended schedule/command is active and its latest run succeeded within 48 hours. Missing, running, failed or stale evidence remains unhealthy. Establish an actual scheduler success at rollout before declaring the new observation healthy. No raw provider event, account identity or credential appears in its alerts. Fixture checks do not establish real scheduler execution or email delivery.

Before activating the updated helper, configure the same independent `BUILDER_HOSTED_BILLING_KEY` in its private environment and the `builder-billing` function. It must contain 32 random bytes encoded as 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters. The CLI rejects a missing or malformed value; the function rejects unsigned observations. This key is separate from Stripe and database service credentials, is never supplied to website builds, and has not been provisioned by these fixture checks. Deploy migrations through `202609150008_builder_billing_retention.sql` and the matching function before switching the helper runtime. Configure both release destinations with `BUILDER_RELEASE_PROJECT_ID` and the existing service credential as described in [release operations](website-releases.md#l6-output-usage-configuration-implementation-pending-rollout).

No real Stripe account, key, price or subscription has been inspected, created or changed for this implementation. Fixture prices and signed fixture events prove local behavior only. Existing credentials may be available elsewhere; their presence has not yet been established.
