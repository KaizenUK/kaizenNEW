Written 2 October 2026. Read with the task board, marketing skills, site profile and voice-of-customer notes.

## Audience and job

Midland speaks to trade and industrial owners whose useful products are hard to find online. Helen speaks to local service owners whose phone visitors struggle to book. Both visitors know a rebuild is an option and want evidence that it can help their business. These are client-proof pages, not competing service hubs. Keep their URLs and client-specific search titles.

## Ranked messages and evidence

1. Show what changed for a real business. Midland's existing before/after screenshots and six product walkthroughs are first-party project evidence. Helen's approved stock photo stays and remains labelled as an example, never a screenshot or photograph of her premises.
2. Put the result beside the work. The profile approves Midland's load time (over 8 seconds to 1.2 seconds), three times the enquiries in the first month, 40+ years, several genuine enquiries a day and anonymous national interest. Helen's approved result is steady bookings from new local clients without an ongoing monthly SEO bill. Do not imply either outcome is guaranteed.
3. Explain the useful change. Midland buyers can start with their industry, use the oil finder and find services or data sheets. Keep the oil finder's "on licence" wording. Helen's existing story is the route from social media or search to the salon and booking.
4. Offer a free first conversation, with the shared contact action and exact reply sentence. Paid work follows an agreed price and non-refundable deposit. This process is evidenced in the profile and pledge.

## Objections and placement

| Concern | Answer and location |
| --- | --- |
| We paid for a poor site before | Actual work and approved results near the start; descriptive pledge link beside the closing action |
| Our business is different | Name each client's business type in the first screen; explain its own buying or booking problem |
| The new site might only look better | Show the industry route and oil finder, retain deeper examples in initial-HTML disclosures; explain Helen's booking route |
| Will I have to learn everything? | Link to the service choices and explain that the first conversation is about what needs to change |
| What happens when I ask? | The agreed reply sentence below each main action; free conversation before paid work |

The hierarchy is an evidence-informed design hypothesis, not a measured conversion finding. Future enquiry quality, owner feedback and Search Console history can test it after the pages have had time to run.
