# P-09: contract product owner page brief

Prepared 2 October 2026 during the P-07 release. Implementation remains next on the board.

## Reader and job

A business owner has a software project that keeps costing money, lacks a clear next step or needs decisions across several suppliers. They know they need help but may confuse planning delivery with choosing what the product should do. Explain the service in Kaizen's voice, with Sean's name and existing experience as the proof, as agreed in DEC-07.

The page owns commercial contract-product-owner and software-project-rescue intent. Keep `/contract-product-owner/`, its search fields and the existing link to the merged fix-versus-rebuild guide. The author page supports identity; the guide helps readers diagnose the problem without duplicating the commercial page.

## Ranked messages and evidence

1. Give the project a clear next step. Existing page copy offers a review of the current work, agreed aims and priorities. This is the service offer, not a guarantee that every project can be saved.
2. You work with Sean McDonnell, Kaizen's founder. This is the approved page voice and existing biography. Link the author profile and its company-record evidence. Do not add a photo or new current credentials.
3. Keep the purpose and plan connected. Retain the useful distinction between choosing what to build and planning how it gets done, using two balanced modules rather than red-versus-green scoring. This is a service explanation, not evidence that either role is inferior.
4. Show relevant work. Existing experience at Playtech, High 5 Games, SMD Credit Solutions and LeoVegas can retain its existing specific descriptions. These are Sean's stated work history, not claims that those businesses are Kaizen clients or endorsements. The real Midland project demonstrates buyer-led choices; use its approved story and screenshot without recasting it as a software-rescue engagement.
5. Offer the agreed contact action and reply. Explain the free first conversation, agreed scope and price before paid work. Link the pledge and the software guide for owners who need to think further.

## Objections and placement

| Owner's concern | Answer and placement |
| --- | --- |
| More money might be wasted | Lead with reviewing the work and choosing the useful next step; link the fix-versus-rebuild guide |
| What does a product owner actually do? | Short purpose-versus-plan explanation near the offer, followed by the review/prioritise/check process |
| Why trust this service? | Sean's linked identity, unchanged concrete experience and clearly identified real Midland work |
| Will this replace existing suppliers? | Preserve the explicit offer to work alongside current suppliers with agreed scope |
| What happens if I ask? | Exact reply under the main action and a free first conversation; commitments explained before paid work |

The order is an evidence-informed hypothesis based on the founder brief and current page. Later enquiry quality and owner feedback can test it. Do not invent project saves, savings, timelines, quotes, credentials or endorsements.

## Design and proof boundaries

Use F foundations and standard page margins. Keep the H1 to two deliberate lines at desktop width, with a clear mobile reading order and the contact action/reply in the first phone screen. Show real work in a browser frame. Avoid a large empty half of the hero, decorative gradients, repeated sales sections or duplicated mobile/desktop copy. Keep useful detail available in initial HTML and use descriptive headings.

Use Sean's existing work-history descriptions without expanding duties or adding dates. The legacy PRINCE2/PMI/HubSpot labels have no current verification in this task's evidence; omit the standalone credential panel instead of presenting it as checked. Do not imply the company-record link verifies employment history. No third-party logo or new testimonial is needed.
