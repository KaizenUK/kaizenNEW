## Reader and job

An owner has a slow or dated WordPress site, or wants a new one. They need to understand whether to improve it, rebuild it or keep WordPress for a fresh site. This is the commercial owner of WordPress web-design terms in S-01. Keep its existing URL and search fields unless verification finds a specific error.

The main doubt is paying again and still getting a poor site; the next is having to learn a new editor. Both come from Sean's interview. The existing Q-02 copy already answers much of this in plain language. The remaining problem is its presentation: a text-only hero, repeated descriptions of the same three choices, multiple sales endings and proof without a picture of the work.

## Message and evidence order

1. Help with a WordPress site that no longer does its job. Keep the search term clear in the H1, with a real Midland screen nearby.
2. A free first conversation, the agreed contact action and exact response sentence. No free design or draft; work starts after the agreed scope and non-refundable deposit.
3. Show why the work can be trusted. Midland's approved result is more than eight seconds to 1.2 seconds and three times the enquiries in the first month. Place the result beside the real work, not in a detached claim band.
4. Three distinct choices with enough buying detail to compare them. Use the existing service descriptions; combine repeated explanation where it saves reading without losing a useful distinction.
5. Answer the editor and launch concerns: pages can be edited, the work is shown before launch, the same person handles the conversation and changes can be made for the owner. Link the pledge and the existing comparison guide.
6. Retain the ten useful FAQ answers in initial HTML with matching data. One clear closing action.

The promise of better enquiries is a service aim, not a guaranteed result for every customer. Do not imply a rebuild is always needed, guarantee search positions or add prices, timing claims, credentials or client quotes.
