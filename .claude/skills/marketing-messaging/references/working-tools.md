# Working tools

Adapted in our own words from the Copy Hackers worksheets (Product Positioning Document, Amazon Review Mining, Content and Tone Audit) and the Making Websites Win tables (O/CO, gap analysis of visitor intentions, the funnel planner). Use the parts a job needs. Keep them in the task's notes, an issue comment or an audit file, not on the page.

## 1. Page brief (shared by the messaging, design and SEO skills)

- **Page and job:** the URL, and the one thing it must get the visitor to do or believe.
- **Who:** the segment (from the site profile). Who we are *not* writing for.
- **What they already know:** problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware; how they arrive (search term, ad, email, link).
- **Target query and intent** (from `seo-strategy`): the term this page owns and the question journey around it.
- **What brought them here:** the trigger, in their words if we have them.
- **Ranked messages:** 3 to 7 lines, each marked *evidence* (with source) or *hypothesis*.
- **Objection map:** see 3. Mark where on the page each is answered.
- **Proof available:** see 4.
- **Next step:** the primary action, and the smaller step for someone not ready.
- **What would show it worked:** the analytics event, reply rate or test we will watch.

## 2. Feature and benefit inventory (after the CH product positioning document)

| Feature | Unique to us? (or who else has it) | Pain it solves, in customer words | Benefit (direct / higher-level) | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|

List every feature before writing; do not polish words here. Drop rows with no real benefit. Priority follows how often the pain comes up in the evidence.

## 3. Objection and counter-objection table (after MWW O/CO and CH ch8)

| Objection (as the visitor would say it) | How often / source | Counter-objection | Proof | Where it is answered (page, section, FAQ, chat) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Seed with the eight common objections (no need, no authority, don't want to be sold to, other priorities, current way works, doubt capability, price, convincing others) and replace with real ones. Good counter-objections come from how the owner and support already answer on chat and calls.

## 4. Proof inventory

| Claim | Proof we have | Where it sits now | Proof we could earn |
|---|---|---|---|

Allowed proof is listed in the site profile (testimonials with permission, official listings, real screens and work, verified facts with a source). Everything in the last column is a task, not copy.

## 5. Voice-of-customer log (after the CH review-mining sheet)

| Memorable phrase (verbatim) | What they want | What they are angry about or fear | Source and date | Times seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Sources while there are few customers: the owner's call notes and how they pitch, existing customers, trade forums and groups, trade press comments, published regulator or industry FAQs, reviews of competitor or adjacent tools, Search Console queries, early chats. Add support tickets and customer surveys once customers exist. Count repeats; frequency is the first test of a phrase. Dated entries go in the site's `docs/marketing/voice-of-customer.md`.

## 6. Competitor message audit (after the CH content audit and the tone deck)

Per competitor (home page plus one product page): value proposition; top messages in the order they appear; primary calls to action (wording, position, look); reasons to believe; tone (how would you describe it, who does it seem aimed at, does it match the visual design?). Then two tallies across all sites: messages by frequency (common = expected or white noise), calls to action by frequency. End with one line: what nobody is saying that our buyers care about.

## 7. Visitor intention gap check (after MWW p184-188)

| What visitors came to do | Share of visits | Do we satisfy it? | How could we? |
|---|---|---|---|

Source: an on-page question "What did you come here to do today?", chat openers, site search. Feeds product and content ideas, not only copy.

## 8. Funnel steps (after the MWW funnel planner)

| What the visitor wants at this step | What we want | How we give both |
|---|---|---|

Walk it from first touch to the first real result the customer gets. Every step should leave the visitor slightly better off.

## Survey and interview questions worth reusing

- Prospects and early users (few or no customers yet): "What's going on in your business that made you look at this?" (CH p16); "How do you handle [the job] today, and what goes wrong?"; "What would stop you switching?"; "Who else would need to agree?" (CH p60-61).
- Customers, once there are some: "What nearly stopped you signing up?" (MWW p102); "What was going on that made you look for something like this?" (CH p34); "If you had ten words to describe us to someone like you, what would you say?" (CH p34); "What would you miss most if it went?" (CH p34); "Where did you first hear about us?" (MWW p339).
- Visitors: "What did you come here to do today?"; "Were you able to do it?"; if not, "What stopped you?" (MWW p107).
- Interviews: one person, recorded, open questions, let them talk, push past the first answer (CH p32).

## Founder or sales interview (question bank)

Ask one person at a time and let them talk. Record their words, not a summary.
1. Who are you usually speaking to, and who makes the decision?
2. What kinds of businesses are most interested, and roughly how big or busy are they?
3. When [the topic] comes up, what is the first thing they say?
4. How do they do it today?
5. What worries them most?
6. How do you explain what we do, in one or two sentences, the way you say it out loud?
7. What makes them lean in? When do they say "oh, that's useful"?
8. What reasons do they give for "no" or "not yet"?
9. What alternative do they mention, and what do you tell them?
10. When price comes up, what do they expect and how do they react?
11. What does an existing customer get from it day to day, in their words? Anything we may quote?

## Full checklist pass (write it down)

Every audit, rewrite, redesign or new marketing page records a "Full checklist pass" in the site's audit notes (default `docs/audits/`): each item from the three skills with its result. A step that could not run says so and why. Skipping a step silently is the failure this exists to stop.
- **Messaging:** workflow steps 1 to 5 and output (a) to (e); tools 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 (or why it could not run), 7 and 8; the clarity rules and house voice line by line; the voice-of-customer file as the evidence.
- **Design:** the diagnosis table, structure, headings (spoilers, not categorisers), calls to action, mobile researched separately, visual hierarchy, and every item in "Checking a design".
- **SEO:** page type and query type, the page-owner map for every term the page touches, the title/meta/H1 rules, the quality bar (entity clarity, Who/How/Why, YMYL where it applies), the technical guardrails, the people-first check, the redesign checklist before shipping, and a change-log entry.
